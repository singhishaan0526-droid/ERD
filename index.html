Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Chen notation and Crow's Foot notation in ER diagrams?

Peter Chen's 1976 notation represents entities as rectangles, attributes as ovals, and relationships as diamonds with explicit cardinality markers (1:1, 1:N, M:N). It is the canonical notation taught in university computer science and database systems curricula. Crow's Foot notation represents tables as boxes with columns inside and connections as lines with multi-fork symbols. ER Diagram Maker specializes in academic Chen notation while offering 1-click export to SQL DDL.

The built-in SQL parser analyzes tables, columns, primary keys, foreign keys, and constraints, automatically drawing entity rectangles, attribute ovals, and relationship diamonds on the canvas.

How do I model weak entities and partial keys in Chen notation?

A weak entity depends on an owner entity for its existence and primary identification (e.g., OrderItem depending on Order). In Chen notation, weak entities are represented with double rectangles, identifying relationships with double diamonds, and partial keys (discriminators) with dashed underlines. In our tool, click on any entity and toggle 'Weak Entity' to render double borders automatically.
